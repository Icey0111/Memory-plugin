# Long-context LLM behaviour and memory/retrieval evaluation: evidence for AI-roleplay memory design

Scope note: everything tagged [measured] is a reported experimental result; [design claim] is an author's recommendation; [vendor claim] is a capability claim by a model provider. Nothing here tests the human-brain analogy; that is a separate design question.

## Findings

1. **LongMemEval** [measured]: 500 human-written questions over 5 abilities (information extraction, multi-session reasoning, temporal reasoning, knowledge updates, abstention). Two settings: S = ~115k tokens/question (~50 sessions); M = 500 sessions ~1.5M tokens. Answer scoring uses an LLM judge; retrieval is measured with Recall@k and NDCG@k. Offline reading of the full history with GPT-4o scores 0.9184, but commercial assistants with the same model score 0.5773 (ChatGPT, -37%) and 0.3299 (Coze, -64%). Long-context LLMs drop 30-60% vs an oracle-evidence context (Llama 3.1 8B 0.710->0.420; Phi-3-128k 14B 0.722->0.344; Phi-3.5-mini 0.652->0.324).
2. **LoCoMo** [measured]: 50 dialogues, avg 300 turns / 9k tokens / up to 35 sessions, human-verified for long-range consistency. QA uses partial-match F1; event summarization uses FactScore (atomic-fact decomposition). Human overall 87.9 vs GPT-4-turbo 32.1 and GPT-3.5-turbo 22.4. Long-context GPT-3.5-16K improves monotonically with more history (4K 24.1 -> 16K 37.8 overall) but adversarial-answer accuracy collapses (13.1 -> 2.1). Long-context and RAG improve over base by 22-66% yet remain ~56% below human, and 73% below on temporal reasoning.
3. **RULER** [measured]: 17 LMs x 13 tasks in 4 categories (retrieval NIAH variants; multi-hop variable tracking; aggregation/common-word extraction; QA with distractors), 4K-128K. Nearly all models are near-perfect on vanilla NIAH yet degrade sharply with length. The repo's "Effective Length" column (threshold-based; the paper calls it a "qualitative threshold" without publishing a numeric fraction) shows: GPT-4-1106 claimed 128K/effective 64K; Llama 3.1 70B 128K/64K; Llama 3.1 8B 128K/32K; Qwen2-72B 128K/32K. Independent work (STRING) puts Llama 3.1 70B's RULER effective length at 64K and notes most open models are <50% of training length.
4. **Needle-in-a-Haystack (original)** [measured]: single fact inserted at varying depth into Paul Graham essays; near-saturated. RULER explicitly calls it "a superficial form of long-context understanding".
5. **Lost in the Middle** [measured]: multi-document QA and JSON key-value retrieval. Performance is U-shaped in the position of the answer (primacy/recency); with the answer mid-context, GPT-3.5-Turbo falls below its closed-book score (56.1%). Going from 20 to 50 retrieved documents adds only ~1.5% (GPT-3.5) and ~1% (Claude-1.3); extended-context models perform about the same as their non-extended counterparts.
6. **"Context rot"** = Chroma's technical report [measured, with a coined name]: 18 models (GPT-4.1, Claude 4, Gemini 2.5, Qwen3). Performance is non-uniform in length even on a trivial repeated-words task; degradation worsens as needle-question cosine similarity (averaged over 5 embedding models) drops; 0/1/4 distractors compound it; shuffled haystacks beat logically structured ones on average.
7. **Retrieval vs long context** - sources disagree. Self-Route [measured]: on 9 datasets (LongBench + InfinityBench, avg ~100k tokens), full long context beats Contriever top-k RAG by +7.6 (Gemini-1.5-Pro), +13.1 (GPT-4o), +3.6 (GPT-3.5-Turbo) points; cost is RAG's advantage, and Self-Route's self-reflection router [design claim] cuts cost 65%/39%. Databricks [measured]: over 20 models, 2,000->128,000 (2M) tokens, 512-token chunks, text-embedding-3-large, FAISS IndexFlatL2, most models first improve then decline; only a handful hold accuracy past 64k; most open models are effective only to 16k-32k. Long-Context LLMs Meet RAG [measured]: generation rises then falls as retrieved-passage count grows because "hard negatives" (often from stronger retrievers) poison the context; retrieval reordering is a training-free mitigation. Reconciliation: "more context" is not monotone, and the winner depends on model class (frontier closed vs open) and on feeding everything vs adding retrieved chunks.
8. **NoLiMa** [measured]: 12 models claiming >=128K, needles with minimal lexical overlap. At 32K, 10/12 fall below 50% of their short-context baseline; GPT-4o drops 99.3% -> 69.7%.
9. **Persona persistence** [measured]: 100+ round persona dialogues with 7 models; persona fidelity (knowledge, style, in-character consistency) degrades over the dialogue and reverts toward default behaviour, with a fidelity/instruction-following trade-off. This is the nearest roleplay-relevant measurement found.

## What this does NOT establish

- **Fictional-world consistency / contradiction avoidance: unmeasured.** LongMemEval's "knowledge updates" and "abstention" and LoCoMo's "adversarial" category are the closest proxies, but they test a user's facts and refusal-to-answer, not whether a scene contradicts established world state. No cited benchmark scores cross-entity world-state consistency.
- **Persona fidelity is not world consistency.** The one long-roleplay measurement (2512.12775) tracks persona-vs-system-prompt drift, not contradictions among many fictional facts.
- **Cost is barely measured.** Only Self-Route (percent cost reduction) and Databricks (qualitative) report it; the core benchmarks are accuracy-only, with no $/turn or latency-under-interaction budget.
- **Determinism: unmeasured.** No benchmark reports run-to-run variance; LongMemEval and LoCoMo scoring are themselves LLM-based (judge / FactScore), adding stochasticity, and decoding settings are not varied in the reported numbers.
- **Retrieval robustness on roleplay registers is unmeasured.** LoCoMo notes retrievers trained for semantic similarity do badly on dialogue with anaphora; NoLiMa shows literal-match shortcuts inflate scores. Jargon-heavy fictional lore is untested.
- **The central design premise is untested here.** Nothing in this literature shows that non-brain-like (lossless, append-only, consistency-first) memory is better for roleplay. That remains a design claim.
